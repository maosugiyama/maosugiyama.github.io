Cyrillic Forest Walk with Cats

ねこと学ぶキリル文字 — ロシア語アルファベットの森の散歩

A free, open web textbook of Russian built for Japanese-speaking learners — a walk through a forest where 33 hand-drawn cats introduce the 33 letters of the Cyrillic alphabet, and the path continues all the way to participles, gerunds and the subjunctive.

Please note: the language of instruction is Japanese. The explanations rely on Japanese–Russian contrast (particles vs. cases, Japanese loan-word phonetics, and so on), so the app is most useful to readers of Japanese. This page exists so that colleagues and researchers abroad can see what the project is.

What is inside

  • The alphabet — 33 letters, each with an original cat illustration, a mnemonic word, an example sentence, audio, and a spelling quiz.
  • Phonics — final devoicing, vowel reduction, assimilation, hard/soft consonants, stress minimal pairs (за́мок/замо́к) with listen-and-compare buttons.
  • 52 grammar units — noun gender to the six cases, verbal aspect, verbs of motion with prefixes, participles, gerunds, conditional бы; plus vocabulary units (colours, clothing, seasons, character) illustrated by the author.
  • 563 exercises, where every wrong option has its own explanation — not only why the answer is right, but why each distractor is wrong.
  • Spaced repetition of missed items, dictation mode, a paper-notebook method with printable A4 worksheets, and an offline-capable PWA.
  • A hand-drawn map of the curriculum as a forest — learners see where they are walking, day-by-day footprints included.

Design commitments

  • All illustrations and photographs are original work by the author.
  • No ads, no account, no paywall; learning data stays on the device and is shared with the author only if the learner explicitly submits it.
  • Accessibility: WCAG AA contrast throughout, lang="ru" tagging for screen readers, keyboard focus indicators.

For researchers and teachers

The app is part of a study on materials development for Russian-language education in Japan. It is released under CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 — free to use and share in classrooms with attribution; contact the author for other uses.
